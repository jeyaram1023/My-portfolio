📡 Relay Attacks (BLE / RFID / NFC)

High Severity

Attackers use dual-transceiver bridge devices to extend signal distance between a valid key badge (inside a pocket or office) and reader locks at EV vehicles or Server Room doors.

Failure: Absence of physical distance verification in BLE/NFC protocols.

🔄 Replay & RollJam Attacks

High Frequency

Intercepting authentication payloads over Sub-GHz or BLE and re-transmitting them later. RollJam uses reactive jamming to capture unused valid rolling codes from door remotes and vehicle fobs.

Failure: Inadequate challenge-response or predictable rolling code algorithms.

🛠️ Drill, Glitch & Solenoid Bypass

Physical Critical

Physically drilling small access points into electronic lock casings or server rack enclosures to reach internal wires, short solenoids, or perform power glitching directly on key-controller MCUs.

Failure: Absence of active casing tamper detection & power monitoring circuitry.

Cross-Domain Synthesis & Literature Gaps

Literature Survey Synthesis across Access Domains

A systematic analysis of contemporary literature (2018–2024) reveals a profound structural vulnerability in physical access control architectures across multiple industries. In Automotive and EV Smart Keys [1], [2], wireless conveniences such as Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) and Passive Keyless Entry (PKE) expose drivers to silent distance extension through relay attacks. Software-layer encryption fails because attackers do not decrypt payload data; they merely amplify and bridge the RF medium across several hundred meters.

Extending this review to Data Center and Server Room Security [3], [7], Near Field Communication (NFC) and RFID corporate badges present identical vulnerabilities. Attackers utilizing handheld skimmers or relay malware installed on smartphone devices can relay access credentials from an employee in a cafeteria directly to a server room door reader in real time.

Crucially, in the domain of Commercial Smart Locks and Industrial Safety Enclosures [4], [5], literature highlights a severe blind spot regarding physical tampering. While network security receives heavy attention, physical lock casings routinely fail against targeted drill attacks, magnetic solenoid manipulation, and power fault injection (glitching). Drilling into key microcontrollers allows adversaries to directly intercept internal UART/CAN lines or ground enable pins, rendering upstream cryptographic handshakes completely moot.

Literature Survey Finding

Current research repeatedly demonstrates that software encryption on broadcast wireless mediums or unmonitored physical enclosures cannot guarantee access security. There is an urgent literature demand for tamper-aware, localized, physically verifiable authentication frameworks.

🚫 Wireless Protocol Patching Band-Aids

Literature heavily focuses on applying complex software cryptography on inherently vulnerable wireless mediums (BLE/Wi-Fi/NFC), rather than addressing the broadcast medium itself as the critical single point of failure.

🚫 Vulnerability to Drill & Casing Breaches

Most smart door lock research evaluates digital network exploits while ignoring physical bypasses. Standard electronic locks have zero active impedance against precision drilling or casing destruction.

🚫 Unreliable Distance-Bounding Mechanisms

Software approaches like Time-of-Flight (ToF) and RSSI distance estimation suffer high error rates in concrete server rooms and metallic garage environments, frequently locking out legitimate users.

🚫 Heavy Computational Overhead on Edge MCUs

Proposed AI intrusion detection models in literature are often designed for cloud servers, making them too power-hungry for low-power battery microcontrollers inside access door handles.
